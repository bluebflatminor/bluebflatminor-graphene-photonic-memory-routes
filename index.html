Epistemic status No experimental prototype combining graphene gating, ferroelectric polarization, and photonic waveguide operation has been demonstrated. The Goldilocks window, route rankings, and crossover thresholds are falsifiable theoretical predictions derived from a device architecture that does not yet exist. The four experiments in Section 7 are the falsification protocol, not supplementary work. The author does not have access to the experimental infrastructure required to execute those experiments. This is a call to the field.
§ 2 — Quality window

The Goldilocks Window

Five simultaneous criteria derived from the Kubo optical conductivity formalism and a 0.5 dB total insertion loss budget for a 64×64 MVM tile. All five must pass jointly. The binary gate model is a conservative screening approximation; Kubo-level compensation between parameters yields approximately +2–5 pp on Route I's joint probability without changing the route ranking.

Parameter Symbol Threshold (64×64) Physical basis
Defect density I_D/I_G < 0.1 Carrier scattering, conductivity modulation depth
Domain size L_domain > 10 µm Grain boundary insertion loss variance across optical mode
Carrier mobility µ > 3,000 cm²/V·s Drude intraband absorption vs. electro-optic modulation
Carrier density n* < 10¹¹ cm⁻² Pauli blocking contrast, Fermi level offset under ferroelectric switching
Monolayer coverage P(mono) > 95% area Bilayer islands reduce optical modulation depth below 5% of single-layer value

Array scaling: n* threshold scales as 1/N. At 16×16 the threshold relaxes 4×; at 128×128 it tightens 2×. The single-crystal substrate requirement is array-size dependent — holds above N ≈ 64.

Below this mobility mean: Route I is the rational integration choice. Above it: Route F is superior on every dimension simultaneously — no transfer step, in-place pre-qualification, thermal co-management through the diamond underlayer. This is a measurable process development target, not a qualitative substrate preference.

§ 7 — Falsification protocol

The Four Experiments

Ordered by decision value. Each is designed to produce a specific number that updates a prior and resolves a design choice. These are not supplementary work — they are the falsification targets the framework was built to specify.

1
Route F Mobility Mapping

Hall mobility on SCD(111) Ni-assisted graphene (Ni 0.5–5 nm, RTA 850–1050°C) before and after H-plasma treatment. The Route F mobility prior is anchored to arXiv 2022 — a single-point failure risk. If that anchor is optimistic by a factor of 2, the crossover threshold shifts materially and the long-term architecture target changes.

Post-H-plasma mobility mean + distribution → update Route F prior µ_ln, σ_ln
2
Highest Priority
Route J Transfer Penalty — Validate or Eliminate

Transfer CVD/Pt graphene to three substrates in parallel: (a) SCD(111) bonded via 5/10/20 nm SiO₂ interlayers; (b) P(VDF-TrFE) ferroelectric dry transfer (Zhang et al. 2025); (c) hBN hot pick-up reference. Measure Hall mobility and carrier density before and after. Route J rests on a ~10× carrier density transfer penalty with no direct experimental measurement. This experiment either validates the bridge route or eliminates it.

Measured transfer penalty → replace ~10× estimate · resolve SCD(111) infrastructure decision
3
Raman Strain–Doping Decomposition

Develop and validate a Raman protocol separating compressive strain (G-peak blue-shift) from carrier doping (2D-peak red-shift and asymmetry) at each integration stage. No current ISO/IEC standard covers the substrate-dependent doping offset parameter required for ferroelectric photonic device integration. Without this, Experiments 1, 2, and 4 report carrier density with systematic error.

Validated decomposition protocol → quantitative prior-updating instrument for the Bayesian framework
4
Spatial Carrier Density Mapping

KPFM or scanning photocurrent microscopy over ≥50×50 µm² at sub-micron resolution. Measure spatial correlation length L_corr at each integration stage including post-ferroelectric switching cycle. L_corr is a coupled graphene-ferroelectric metric: graphene modifies HZO domain structure (Chen et al. 2025), so L_corr post-switching encodes the domain engineering effect directly.

L_corr → determines calibration strategy · replaces P_array independence bound with correlation-aware model
§ 6.1 — Practical strategy

Three-Phase Entry Strategy

No redesign of the photonic stack is required between phases. Each phase builds on the same SCD(111) substrate infrastructure.

Phase 1 — Now
Route I

CVD/Pt → hBN encapsulation. Available today with commercial suppliers. 38.6% per-cell pass probability. Begin device demonstrations.

Phase 2 — Bridge
Route J

CVD/Pt → SCD(111) bonded substrate. When diamond infrastructure is in place. Pending Experiment 2 validation of the transfer penalty prior.

Phase 3 — Long-term
Route F

SCD(111) Ni+H-term in-place graphitization. When the 3,500–4,500 cm²/V·s crossover threshold is cleared by process development. Up to 57.2% at the H-termination ceiling.

Key literature

Primary Anchors

Reference Role in framework
Xu et al., Nature Comms 16, 2025 Pockels photonic memory — 6 optical states, femtojoule-per-state energy, 10⁷-cycle endurance. Primary ferroelectric photonic memory reference.
Chen et al., Nanoscale, July 2025 Graphene intercalated in HZO FeFET — >10⁸ cycle endurance via domain engineering and charge injection suppression. Geometric caveat: intercalated, not above-ferroelectric.
Patil et al., Small, Oct 2025 Graphene planar electrode on HZO — sub-fJ switching (~0.85 fJ), 3D vertical geometry. Closest published device geometry to above-ferroelectric optical gate.
Zhang et al., Adv. Mater. 37(50), 2025 P(VDF-TrFE) ferroelectric dry transfer for CVD graphene — electrostatically switchable adhesion, PMMA-free, CMOS-compatible. Route I improvement candidate in Experiment 2.
Banszerus et al., Sci. Adv. 1, 2015 Route I hBN encapsulation transfer prior anchor — ~5× carrier density penalty, 40–60% mobility retention for large-area CVD graphene.
Gao et al., Nature Comms 3, 2012 CVD/Pt growth prior anchor — millimetre-scale grains, 20–50 µm domains under optimised AP-CVD. Growth priors for Routes G, I, J.
Sumant et al., Nature Comms 7, 2016 UNCD Ni-assisted graphitization — peak mobility 2,587 cm²/V·s, Route D anchor. Establishes domain size as the substrate physics wall for all UNCD routes.
